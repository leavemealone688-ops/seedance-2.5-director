# Viral Structure: Hooks, Retention, Twists, Endings and Data

> The seconds and ratios here are common vertical-drama practice, not platform-published standards. Calibrate with your own analytics (section 9).

## 1. The logline comes first

Before the script, write one line: `genre tag × core twist`. The twist is **the specific promise of this show**, not clichés like "face-slapping comeback":
- "The number that calls every midnight is the phone she canceled ten years ago."
- "Only she can see the village well — and someone inside it is calling her name."

The title, cover and first line all grow out of this sentence.

## 2. Hooks in the first 1-3 seconds

Something happens in frame one. Hook types:

| Type | Example | How to prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Conflict already happening | A slap just landed; divorce papers already stamped | `0-1s: open mid-action, …`, no wind-up |
| Visual spectacle | The floor splits into an abyss; a whole room turns at once | Frame 0 is the spectacle at its biggest |
| Counter-intuitive line | "The wedding's off — the groom is my dad." | Line in 0-2 s, first word spoken at once |
| Danger countdown | A finger a hair away from the ink pad | Close-up + very slow push |
| Status gap | A delivery rider publicly addressed as "Chairman" | Show the gap first, then the reaction |
| Mystery object | A blank surrender scroll; a bell that won't ring | Open on a prop close-up |

Never: empty establishing shots, slow walk-ins, sky shots, title cards.

## 3. What must land in the first 30 seconds

- An **irreversible fact**: the thumbprint is pressed, the taboo is triggered, the trap has closed.
- An **information gap**: the audience knows what the characters don't (or the reverse) — fuel for bingeing.
- The lead's **goal** is clear and the first layer of resistance shows.

## 4. Retention rhythm

- Give a **small payoff or twist every 10-20 seconds** (new info, a turn, a sharp line); never 20+ seconds of pure setup.
- Contrast: short fast shots in conflict, 1-2 s of breath at emotional landings.
- Each scene advances one thing; never re-explain the same information.
- Short lines: one piece of information per line; if an action can say it, don't speak it.
- At generation level: one 10-20 s prompt = one "small payoff unit"; mark the payoff second in the beat table.

## 5. Twists and payoffs

- **Setup → payoff**: any prop / line / ability shown early must be used later (e.g. a bell that won't ring in the opening → at the end it rings by itself as a warning).
- Twists need planted clues that hold up on rewatch; clueless twists feel like cheating.
- Measurable satisfaction: pick a meter by genre (power gap, how many people know the secret, a countdown, counted acts of care) and pay it off at least once per episode.
- Shooting a twist: reaction first, cause second; or show half the cause and let the audience guess.

## 6. Episode-end hooks and CTA

End-hook types:
- Threat unresolved (the person outside keeps pounding on the door)
- Identity about to be revealed (she looks up at that light)
- Misunderstanding escalates
- A resource about to be won / lost
- Danger after sweetness (an uneasy detail right after a warm moment)

Rules:
- The binge question at the end must share roots with the opening logline; don't switch to an unrelated question midway.
- End on **the frame where the question is raised**, not on staring after the answer.
- Put CTAs (follow, next episode) in the caption or a 1 s end card — never have a character tell the camera to follow; it breaks immersion.
- In the prompt: the last beat ends at the action that raises the question; "the frame holds still" in the final 0.5 s leaves an edit point.

## 7. Vertical-drama episode structure (reference)

| Section | Share of runtime (rule of thumb) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | First 3 s | Conflict / spectacle / counter-intuitive line |
| Setup | By 15-20 s | Irreversible fact + info gap + goal |
| Escalation | Middle | 2-3 small payoffs / twists |
| Peak | Last quarter | The episode's biggest payoff or twist |
| End hook | Last 3-5 s | A new question |

Break into generation units: one unit per 10-20 s, each with a beat table (`../templates/script-to-shots.md`).

## 8. Covers and titles

- Cover: 9:16, the strongest conflict frame or a sharp rebuild; 3-8 big words of conflict or suspense; big, readable face; text clear of platform UI.
- Titles weld **event + relationship / arena** together: "A delivery rider brings the wrong order — straight into his own boardroom."
- No ending spoilers, no cover that misrepresents the episode (it raises skip rate and hurts the account).
- Prepare 2-3 cover / title versions per episode and let the data pick.

## 9. Iterate with data

Metrics to watch (names vary by platform):
- **Skip / swipe-away in the first 2-3 s**: high → weak hook; change frame one or the first line.
- **Average watch time / completion rate**: low → find where the retention curve drops.
- **Steep drops in the retention curve**: map them to the clip and second in the storyboard — usually overlong setup, staring, or repeated information.
- **Engagement (comments, shares)**: see which line or twist people discuss; push it further next episode.
- **Click-through (cover / title)**: compare cover versions.

Method:
1. After each release, log: title, cover version, runtime, what happens in the first 3 s, each metric.
2. Find the second where retention drops → the storyboard row → write a "don't do this again" rule.
3. Change one variable at a time (only the opening, or only the cover), or you won't know what worked.
4. Fold what works back into this pack's templates and rules.
5. Use real analytics only — never invented or estimated numbers.

## 10. Copy-ready checklist

1. Frame one has action or a line; the first word lands within 1 s.
2. An irreversible fact lands within 30 s.
3. A small payoff every 10-20 s.
4. Every planted prop gets used later.
5. End the episode the moment a new question appears.
6. Cover = the episode's strongest conflict frame + 3-8 words of suspense.
7. The title welds event + relationship.
8. Log data every episode; change one variable at a time.
