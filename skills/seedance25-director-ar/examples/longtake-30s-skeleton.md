# One-take skeleton up to 30 s (structural sketch, not a rendered prompt)

For platforms with long durations such as TapNow / Higgsfield; a 16:9 trailer dinner-table scene.

```
{{Image 1}} is the start frame: it locks composition, seating, and props. {{Image 2}} is the blonde woman in a black turtleneck: same face and hair. {{Audio 1}} is only the blonde woman's voice timbre, nobody else's.
14 seconds, 16:9, one continuous take from a pantry doorway into a candlelit dining room, with a single slow forward glide.

0-2s: the diners make small natural movements. End state: the camera holds the start-frame composition.
2-5s: the old man at the head of the table says: "Your father left the house to the gardener. The votes are mine." At the same time the man on the right scrapes frosting off the cake with his finger. Everyone else keeps their mouth closed. End state: the cake plate has not moved.
5-7s: quiet; the man at the sideboard keeps eating. End state: the camera has not moved.
7-9s: the camera glides forward about 40 cm on a track at an even speed, the frame edges closing in continuously; the whole table and all four seated people stay in frame. End state: the camera stops closer and locks.
9-12s: the blonde woman passes the sheet of paper over the teacup to the slim woman, who lays it flat to the left of her phone; the teacup does not move. The blonde woman says: "Sign." Everyone else keeps their mouth closed.
12-14s: everyone settles; stillness.

Throughout: the doorframe, the small camcorder with its red light, and all four people stay in frame. No subtitles. No music, no BGM, no score. Only the written dialogue, a low fridge hum, soft fork clinks, and one distant laugh cut short.
```

Key points:
- Whole seconds, back to back, one thing per beat + an end state.
- The push-in is written as a physical process inside one beat; no SHOT / CUT / CAMERA PUSH labels anywhere.
- Lines sit in the beat where they happen, with everyone else's mouths closed.
- Visual exclusions become positive "stays in frame" states; negatives only for subtitles and music.
- Extending to 20-30 s: add 3-4 more beats and restate prop positions every 2-3 beats.
